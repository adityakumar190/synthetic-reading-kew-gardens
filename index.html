Synthetic Reading: Kew Gardens

A digital humanities project exploring computational literary analysis

Virginia Woolf

About the Project

This project explores how an AI language model can identify nature imagery, sensory description, human-nature relationships, and narrative techniques in Virginia Woolf's short story Kew Gardens.

Research Question

How are nature imagery, sensory description, and narrative perspective represented in Kew Gardens, and how effectively can an LLM identify these features?

Research Method

The project uses partial reading, prompt-based AI annotation, JSON validation, Python-based analysis, and interactive visualisation. The AI-generated annotations are treated as interpretations that require human review.

Theoretical Approach

Ecocriticism provides a framework for examining representations of the natural environment, while narratology helps explore perspective and the representation of thought. The analysis considers both the potential and the limitations of AI-assisted literary interpretation.
